# Unit 6 Assessment Evidence Map

## Purpose

This document is a teacher-facing overview of where the 17-week curriculum **prepares learners** for the Unit 6 assessment criteria.

It is not a marking decision and does not mean that completing a weekly activity automatically achieves a criterion. Achievement depends on the learner's own evidence produced in response to the approved assignment brief.

## Learning Aim A

| Criterion | Curriculum preparation | Typical evidence focus |
|---|---|---|
| **A.P1** | Weeks 1–4 | Comparison of website design principles, audience and purpose using specific evidence |
| **A.M1** | Weeks 3–4 | Analysis of how design principles and performance factors influence effectiveness and client/user requirements |
| **A.D1** | Week 4 | Balanced evaluation of design, creativity, performance, requirements and organisational impact |

### Learning Aim A progression

**identify → compare → analyse → evaluate**

Learners should move beyond feature spotting to cause-and-effect reasoning and ultimately supported judgement.

## Learning Aim B

| Criterion | Curriculum preparation | Typical evidence focus |
|---|---|---|
| **B.P2** | Weeks 5–7 | Requirements analysis, initial/alternative designs, site structure, wireframes, responsive planning, assets and technical documentation |
| **B.P3** | Weeks 7–8 | Review of design ideas with others, recorded feedback, decisions and refinements |
| **B.M2** | Weeks 6–10 | Justification of design decisions against user needs, client requirements, fitness for purpose and credible alternatives |

### Learning Aim B progression

**brief → requirements → design → alternatives → review → refine → justify**

Strong design evidence should make it possible to trace a final decision back to a requirement or user need.

## Learning Aim C

| Criterion | Curriculum preparation | Typical evidence focus |
|---|---|---|
| **C.P4** | Weeks 9–12 | Development of a functioning website using appropriate HTML, CSS, assets and client-side scripting techniques |
| **C.P5** | Weeks 9 and 12–14 | Testing against requirements, expected/actual results, defects, compatibility, user-related checks, fixes and retesting |
| **C.P6** | Weeks 14–15 | Review of the completed solution, strengths, limitations and improvements against purpose/client requirements |
| **C.M3** | Weeks 13–15 | Evidence-led optimisation/refinement using testing and feedback, with before/after evidence and retesting |

### Learning Aim C progression

**implement → check → formally test → diagnose → refine → retest → evaluate**

The strongest evidence preserves development history rather than presenting only a flawless final product.

## Combined B/C distinction criteria

| Criterion | Curriculum preparation | Typical evidence focus |
|---|---|---|
| **BC.D2** | Weeks 14–16 | Overall evaluation of the website against client requirements, user needs, evidence, alternatives, limitations and optimisation outcomes |
| **BC.D3** | Weeks 8, 12–17 | Evidence of individual responsibility, purposeful creativity and effective self-management across design, development and review |

## Evidence chains to encourage

### Design evidence chain

**requirement → design option → selected decision → reason → review feedback → refinement → final justification**

### Development/testing evidence chain

**requirement → implementation → planned test → result → issue → change → retest → conclusion**

### Evaluation evidence chain

**requirement → evidence → consequence → limitation/trade-off → alternative → judgement**

### Professional-practice evidence chain

**problem/target → learner decision → action → evidence → outcome → reflection**

## Evidence integrity

Learners should retain authentic evidence as the project develops. Appropriate evidence can include:

- design documents and revisions;
- feedback records;
- code/version history;
- screenshots showing relevant behaviour;
- test plans and actual results;
- defect/fix/retest records;
- before/after optimisation evidence;
- learner explanations and evaluations;
- project-management or milestone records where genuinely used.

Git/GitHub history can support a development narrative but does not automatically prove authorship, independence or criterion achievement.

Missing historical evidence must not be fabricated retrospectively. A genuine gap should be acknowledged and, where permitted, strengthened only through authentic current evidence.

## Assessment boundary

The Hot Wheels exemplar teaches techniques and methodology. It must not supply completed Hot Beans design documentation, assessed code, test results, optimisation evidence or final evaluative conclusions.
