# Week 4 Lesson Plan – Website Performance and Evaluation

**W/B:** 28 September 2026  
**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Specification focus:** A2 with consolidation of A1  
**Assessment preparation:** A.P1, A.M1, A.D1

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Explain server-side and client-side performance factors.
2. Apply bandwidth, hits, file type, upload/download speed, browser, cache, processor and interactivity concepts to scenarios.
3. Analyse how design and media choices can affect performance.
4. Explain the consequences of performance for users, clients and organisations.
5. Distinguish comparison, analysis and evaluation.
6. Produce balanced, evidence-based evaluative conclusions.

## Key terminology

server-side, client-side, bandwidth, hits, file type, compression, upload speed, download speed, browser, cache, processor speed, interactivity, latency, compatibility, usability, organisational impact, analyse, evaluate, judgement, conclusion, evidence, trade-off.

## Teacher modelling: compare → analyse → evaluate

Use a neutral website example.

### Compare

Identify similarities and differences in how two websites use a principle.

### Analyse

Explain how and why those choices affect creativity, performance, audience, purpose or client requirements.

### Evaluate

Reach a supported judgement about effectiveness, considering strengths, weaknesses, consequences and trade-offs.

A useful sequence is:

**evidence → effect → consequence → alternative/limitation → judgement**

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- provide a server-side/client-side sorting grid
- use cause/effect sentence stems
- limit early scenarios to one variable at a time
- provide an evaluation paragraph scaffold
- allow paired discussion before independent writing
- colour-code comparison, analysis and evaluation statements

### Stretch

- introduce scenarios with multiple interacting causes
- ask learners to rank factors by likely impact
- require consideration of different devices/network contexts
- ask learners to explain trade-offs rather than treating optimisation as absolute
- require nuanced conclusions using qualifiers such as “overall”, “in this context” and “for this audience”

## English and mathematics

**English:** analytical verbs, comparative language, evaluative judgement, paragraph cohesion, evidence-led conclusions.

**Mathematics:** data size, bandwidth, transfer rate, proportion and relative performance. Use simple illustrative relationships rather than unnecessary calculation unless appropriate to the group.

## EDI / British Values / employability

Discuss the impact of poor performance on users with older devices, slower connections or assistive technologies. Reinforce inclusive access and the professional responsibility to make reasoned design decisions. Link evaluation to professional quality assurance and client communication.

## Misconceptions to anticipate

- server-side automatically means slow
- client-side automatically means fast
- more bandwidth guarantees good performance
- all large files are inappropriate
- cache is always beneficial
- browser problems are the user's fault
- evaluation means listing positives and negatives
- a distinction response is simply longer than a merit response

## Assessment boundary

Do not provide complete BBC/Amazon evaluative conclusions. Model the method elsewhere, then challenge learners to apply it independently.

## After the lesson

Use evidence checklists to identify learners who still need support distinguishing comparison from analysis/evaluation before Assignment 1 final submission.
