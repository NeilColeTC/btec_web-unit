# Week 4 Activity – Website Performance Scenarios

For each scenario:

1. identify the most relevant performance factor(s)
2. classify each factor as mainly server-side, client-side or compatibility-related
3. explain the likely effect on the user
4. suggest an appropriate improvement or mitigation
5. explain any trade-off introduced by your suggestion

## Scenario 1 – Breaking news

A news website normally performs well. During a major breaking story, pages become much slower and some users receive errors.

## Scenario 2 – Large image gallery

A portfolio homepage contains twelve very high-resolution photographs. It looks impressive on the designer's computer but takes a long time to load on mobile data.

## Scenario 3 – Browser problem

An interactive navigation menu works in one browser but fails to open correctly in another.

## Scenario 4 – Returning visitor

A learner notices that a site loads much faster on their second visit than on the first.

## Scenario 5 – Older laptop

A highly animated website feels smooth on a modern computer but becomes sluggish on an older laptop.

## Scenario 6 – Product video

An online shop replaces several static product images with autoplaying high-quality video. Engagement rises for some users, but others leave before the page finishes loading.

## Scenario 7 – Busy application portal

A college application website is responsive most of the year but becomes very slow shortly before an application deadline.

## Scenario 8 – Interactive form

A form provides live validation and helpful instant feedback. On some low-powered devices the page responds slowly while the user types.

## Reflection

Choose one scenario where there is no single perfect solution. Explain the trade-off and make a justified recommendation.
