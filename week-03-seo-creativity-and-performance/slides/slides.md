## Slide 5 – From feature to effect
A feature is not evidence of effectiveness by itself.

Use:
**Feature → Effect → Audience/Purpose**

Today we add:
**Feature → Effect → Audience/Purpose → Performance/Visibility**

## Slide 6 – Position
Position influences:
- what users notice first
- visual hierarchy
- scanning order
- proximity between related items
- prominence of calls to action

Question: where would you place the most important action and why?

---

## Slide 7 – Colour
Colour can support:
- branding
- hierarchy
- emphasis
- meaning
- emotional tone
- recognition

But colour alone should not carry essential meaning.

---

## Slide 8 – Contrast
Contrast helps distinguish:
- text from background
- primary from secondary content
- buttons from surrounding content
- headings from body text

Low contrast may weaken readability and accessibility.

---

## Slide 9 – Size
Size can communicate hierarchy.

Consider:
- heading size
- image size
- button size
- touch targets
- dominant promotional areas

Larger is not automatically better.

---

## Slide 10 – Appropriateness of media
Ask:
1. Does it support the purpose?
2. Is it suitable for the audience?
3. Does it communicate something useful?
4. Does it affect load time?
5. Is it accessible?
6. Is it appropriate on different devices?

## Slide 12 – Creativity and innovation
Creative design may include:
- unconventional layout
- distinctive visual hierarchy
- unusual use of white space
- interactive presentation
- original media choices

Creativity should still support user needs.

---

## Slide 13 – Innovation vs usability
Challenge:
Can a website be visually innovative but difficult to use?

Think about:
- predictability
- navigation conventions
- cognitive load
- accessibility
- audience expectations

---

## Slide 14 – Golden ratio
The golden ratio is approximately **1 : 1.618**.

It can be used as a guide for proportion or layout.

Important: it is a design approach, not a guarantee of quality.

## Slide 16 – What is SEO?
**Search Engine Optimisation** means improving how effectively a website can be discovered and understood by search engines.

It is not the same as paid advertising.

---

## Slide 17 – Indexing
Search engines discover and process pages so they can potentially appear in search results.

Key idea:
**being indexed does not guarantee a high ranking.**

---

## Slide 18 – Meta tags
Meta information can help describe a page to browsers/search services.

Examples may include:
- page description
- document metadata
- information used by search engines

Do not confuse metadata with visible page content.

---

## Slide 19 – Keywords
Keywords should reflect the topic and language users are likely to search for.

Good SEO is not simply repeating a phrase many times.

Relevant, accurate content matters.

---

## Slide 20 – Why updates matter
Regularly maintaining content can:
- keep information accurate
- improve relevance
- remove broken/outdated material
- support user trust
- give search engines current content to process

---

## Slide 21 – Limiting crawling
Not every page necessarily needs to be discovered or indexed.

Website owners may control which areas search engines should or should not crawl.

Why might this be useful?

---

## Slide 22 – Where does processing happen?
Two broad locations:

**Client-side** – code runs on the user's device/browser.

**Server-side** – code runs on the web server before or while content is supplied.

---

## Slide 23 – Client-side examples
Possible client-side uses:
- interface interactions
- input checks
- changing page content after an event
- menus and visual effects

JavaScript commonly runs client-side.

---

## Slide 24 – Server-side examples
Possible server-side uses:
- retrieving stored data
- authenticating users
- processing submitted data
- generating content
- interacting with databases

---

## Slide 25 – Browser compliance
Different browsers may interpret/support technologies differently.

Developers should consider:
- HTML/CSS support
- JavaScript behaviour
- testing across browsers
- graceful handling of unsupported features

---

## Slide 26 – Why compatibility matters
Poor browser compatibility can cause:
- broken layouts
- missing features
- failed interactions
- reduced accessibility
- loss of users or sales

Technical problems can create organisational consequences.

---

## Slide 27 – Compare vs analyse
**Compare:** identifies similarities/differences.

**Analyse:** explains how and why design choices produce effects.

Compare: “Both sites use images, but Amazon uses more product images.”

Analyse: “The denser use of product imagery supports Amazon's transactional purpose by allowing users to compare products quickly, although it also creates greater visual density.”

---
