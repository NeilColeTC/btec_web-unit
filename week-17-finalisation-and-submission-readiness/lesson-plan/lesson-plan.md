# Week 17 Lesson Plan – Finalisation & Submission Readiness

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aims:** B and C  
**Prior learning:** Requirements analysis, design review, implementation, formal testing, optimisation, evaluation and professional-practice review.

## Learning objectives

Learners will:

- verify the final website against major client requirements;
- complete a controlled technical smoke test;
- identify only essential final corrections;
- retest after any final correction;
- organise evidence into a clear submission structure;
- complete an honest final self-audit and reflection.

## Success criteria

### Developing
I can identify the required submission components and check that my website opens and links correctly.

### Secure
I can trace major requirements to implementation and evidence, and I can correct and retest genuine final defects.

### Extending
I can demonstrate a coherent evidence trail across design, implementation, testing, refinement and evaluation while making professional decisions about what **not** to change at the final stage.

## Teacher modelling – the final-week decision rule

Use generic scenarios and ask learners to decide **fix now / record limitation / do not change**.

### Scenario A
A required navigation link is broken and can be repaired quickly.

**Likely response:** fix, retest the link, then regression-test neighbouring navigation.

### Scenario B
A learner wants to add a carousel because another website has one.

**Likely response:** do not add it. No evidenced need; creates unnecessary risk.

### Scenario C
A form label is missing and keyboard/user testing has already identified the issue.

**Likely response:** fix if time permits, then retest form interaction/accessibility.

### Scenario D
A learner notices a major feature was never designed or tested and wants to create it in the final hour.

**Likely response:** do not fabricate a development history. Follow centre assessment rules and record the evidence position accurately.

## Clean-start smoke test

Learners should test the **actual submission copy**, not the working folder they have had open all term.

Suggested process:

1. close the editor/browser;
2. open the final submission folder fresh;
3. launch the intended home page;
4. navigate every required page;
5. check images/assets;
6. test key interaction/form behaviour;
7. check responsive behaviour at representative widths;
8. test keyboard navigation for important controls;
9. inspect console for obvious errors where appropriate;
10. confirm no dependency points outside the submitted folder.

This can reveal path errors and missing assets hidden by the original development environment.

## Evidence organisation

Encourage a simple index rather than a large unstructured dump.

Example headings:

- requirements analysis;
- design documentation;
- review and feedback;
- implementation evidence;
- testing;
- defects and retesting;
- optimisation/refinement;
- evaluation;
- professional-practice reflection.

Evidence should be easy to locate and understand without requiring the assessor to infer what a screenshot or file demonstrates.

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- provide a numbered final-check route;
- allow learners to complete one requirement at a time;
- use Green/Amber/Red evidence status;
- provide a submission-folder naming example;
- pair-check only technical packaging, not assessed conclusions;
- use teacher questioning to help learners locate existing evidence.

### Stretch

Ask learners to:

- identify residual risk in their submission;
- distinguish evidence quality from evidence quantity;
- justify why a possible late change should be rejected;
- audit claim-to-evidence traceability in their final evaluation;
- perform targeted regression checks after the final fix;
- explain which professional working practice most reduced project risk.

## English and maths

### English

Learners should use concise evidence-location language:

> Requirement R3 is implemented in…
>
> Evidence is located in…
>
> Testing demonstrated…
>
> The remaining limitation is…

### Maths/data literacy

Use existing measurable evidence accurately, for example:

- viewport widths;
- number of tests;
- test pass/fail counts;
- file sizes;
- threshold/boundary values.

Do not generate measurements retrospectively if they were never recorded.

## Assessment-safe teacher support

Appropriate prompts include:

- Show me the requirement.
- Where is it implemented?
- What evidence already exists?
- Is this a defect or a new feature idea?
- What could this late change break?
- How will you retest it?
- Are you organising evidence or creating new historical evidence?
- Can an assessor understand this evidence without your verbal explanation?

Avoid writing missing evaluation or historical development evidence for the learner.

## Expected end-of-session evidence

Learners should leave with:

- final requirement verification;
- completed smoke-test record;
- evidence index;
- final corrections and retest evidence where applicable;
- clean submission folder/package;
- final professional reflection;
- completed submission-readiness checklist.
