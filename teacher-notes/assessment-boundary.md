# Assessment Boundary – Teacher Notes

## Core position

The Hot Wheels resource is a **worked teaching exemplar**. Its value is that learners can see a broad range of website-development techniques and a visible development process before undertaking their own assessed work.

It should not become a near-complete assessed solution.

## Safe teaching use

Appropriate activities include:

- explaining exemplar code
- tracing how a feature works
- changing exemplar values/content during practice
- deliberately breaking and repairing exemplar code
- comparing Stage 1, 2 and 3
- identifying programming constructs
- testing exemplar functionality
- critiquing design decisions
- considering alternative implementations
- using Hot Wheels to model how testing can lead to optimisation

## Boundary for the assessed Hot Beans project

Do not use the repository to provide learners with a completed or substantially completed:

- Hot Beans site map
- Hot Beans wireframe/page design
- Hot Beans alternative design
- Hot Beans application-form specification
- Hot Beans pseudocode/flowchart
- Hot Beans JavaScript solution
- Hot Beans test plan/test data
- Hot Beans optimisation issue and fix
- Hot Beans evaluation

Those are areas where the approved assignment requires learner decisions/evidence.

## Reusing techniques versus reusing a solution

Learners should be able to transfer a learned technique.

For example, after studying event listeners and DOM manipulation in Hot Wheels, a learner may independently decide that a different interactive feature is appropriate for their own site.

That is different from taking the Stage 3 site, changing names/images and presenting the result as the assessed response.

## Original client-side scripting

The specification requires original client-side scripting in website development. The exemplar therefore exposes learners to a range of constructs and readable examples, but should not prescribe the functionality that their Hot Beans website must use.

Assessment should consider whether the scripting in the learner's submitted solution is their own purposeful response and whether they can evidence/explain its development.

## Creativity and individual responsibility

BC.D3 concerns individual responsibility, creativity and effective self-management across design, development and review.

The exemplar should therefore broaden learners' technical repertoire while leaving meaningful choices, problem solving, testing outcomes and refinements to the individual learner.

## Evidence integrity

Git history can support discussion of development, but it should not be treated as automatic proof of authorship or criterion achievement. Assessment decisions should be based on the learner's complete evidence and the approved assignment requirements.

## Recommended classroom message

> Learn from the Hot Wheels example, but do not turn Hot Wheels into Hot Beans. Start your assessed solution from the client brief and make your own decisions.
