# Unit 6 Course Delivery – Teacher Quick Start

## What this repository now contains

The repository has two complementary layers:

1. **17-week curriculum delivery framework** — lesson plans, slides, student activities, assessment checks and teacher guidance.
2. **Hot Wheels staged exemplar** — a separate worked website used to investigate and practise transferable development techniques.

These layers should remain distinct. The curriculum teaches the assessed process; the exemplar demonstrates techniques without becoming a Hot Beans solution.

## Starting a teaching week

For each `week-XX-*` folder, use the resources in this order as appropriate:

1. read the weekly `README.md` for curriculum intent and assessment connection;
2. review `lesson-plan/lesson-plan.md` and adapt timings to the group;
3. use `slides/slides.md` as the teaching sequence;
4. select activities from `student-activities/`;
5. use `assessment-checks/` for retrieval, checkpoints and exit evidence;
6. consult `teacher-resources/teacher-guidance.md` for misconceptions, support/stretch and indicative responses.

The Markdown files are deliberately source-format resources so they can be adapted into the presentation/document tooling used by the centre.

## Course phases

### Weeks 1–4 — Learning Aim A

Learners analyse existing websites and develop from comparison to analysis and evaluation.

### Weeks 5–8 — Learning Aim B design cycle

Learners interpret the Hot Beans brief, design independently, develop alternatives, obtain feedback, refine and justify.

### Weeks 9–12 — Learning Aim C implementation

The three Hot Wheels exemplar stages become particularly useful here:

- Week 9: Stage 1 HTML
- Week 10: Stage 2 CSS/responsive design
- Week 11: Stage 3 JavaScript/refinement
- Week 12: consolidate into an independently developed coherent solution

### Weeks 13–14 — Testing and optimisation

Keep failures visible. Learners should show what was tested, what failed, what changed and what the retest demonstrated.

### Weeks 15–17 — Evaluation and finalisation

Shift attention away from new features. Learners organise evidence, evaluate outcomes and working practices, verify the submission copy and close the project professionally.

## Assessment-safe teaching

### Appropriate

- model a process using Hot Wheels or a neutral scenario;
- provide vocabulary, questions, checklists and structural templates;
- demonstrate HTML/CSS/JavaScript techniques in non-assessed contexts;
- ask learners to explain their own choices;
- point learners back to the client brief and their own evidence;
- diagnose missing stages in a process;
- provide formative feedback consistent with centre/Pearson assessment rules.

### Avoid

- completed Hot Beans designs;
- assignment-ready comparative/evaluative paragraphs;
- Hot Beans code that learners can submit as their own;
- manufactured testing or optimisation evidence;
- telling a learner what judgement to make about their own solution;
- treating the Hot Wheels feature set as a checklist of features required for the assessed website.

## Evidence habits to establish early

Encourage learners to keep evidence while they work:

- dated/versioned designs;
- feedback and decisions;
- code versions;
- test results;
- failures and debugging notes;
- before/after evidence;
- retests;
- reasons for changes.

The project becomes much easier to evaluate when learners do not have to reconstruct their process from memory.

## Intervention approach

When a learner is stuck, ask process questions:

- Which requirement are you trying to meet?
- What does your design say should happen?
- What evidence currently shows that?
- What is the smallest failing part?
- What test would prove the behaviour?
- What changed after feedback/testing?
- What limitation remains?

This keeps ownership with the learner while providing meaningful teaching support.

## Quality-assurance principle

The repository should support a learner to answer not just **what did I make?**, but:

> **What requirement was I addressing, why did I make this decision, how did I test it, what changed, and what evidence supports my final judgement?**
