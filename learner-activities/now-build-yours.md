# Now Build Yours

You have investigated the exemplar. The next step is to stop treating Hot Wheels as the design problem.

Return to **your own client requirements**.

The techniques you have studied are tools. Your task is to decide which tools are appropriate for your client's purpose, audience and requirements and then develop your own solution.

## Before you code

Answer these questions from your own brief and planning:

1. What is the purpose of your website?
2. Who are its intended users?
3. What information and functionality do those users need?
4. What pages/content structure best meets those needs?
5. What navigation structure is appropriate?
6. What visual identity is appropriate to this client rather than Hot Wheels?

## Choose techniques deliberately

For your proposed solution, consider:

- Which semantic HTML elements are appropriate?
- Where would Flexbox be useful?
- Where would Grid be useful?
- What needs to change responsively?
- What accessibility considerations affect the design?
- Where would JavaScript genuinely improve the user experience?
- Which features would still need to work without JavaScript?
- How will images and other assets be prepared efficiently?

You do **not** need to use every technique demonstrated by the exemplar.

## Plan evidence of development

Before implementation, decide how you will show your development process.

Consider retaining evidence of:

- initial design decisions
- early implementation
- meaningful changes
- problems encountered
- testing results
- feedback
- improvements made in response to evidence
- retesting
- evaluation against requirements

## Independence check

Before continuing, ask yourself:

> If the Hot Wheels exemplar disappeared now, could I explain why my website looks and works the way it does?

If the answer is yes, you are applying the learning rather than copying the example.

If the answer is no, return to your client requirements and make the decisions your own.

## Final reminder

Your strongest evidence is not that your website contains lots of features. It is that you can demonstrate a coherent process in which requirements lead to design decisions, implementation, testing, improvement and evaluation.
