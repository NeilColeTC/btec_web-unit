# Week 16 Lesson Plan – Distinction Evaluation & Professional Practice

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aims:** B and C  
**Prior learning:** Design documentation and review, HTML/CSS/JavaScript implementation, testing, optimisation and final evaluation.

## Learning objectives

Learners will:

- recognise authentic evidence of individual responsibility, creativity and self-management;
- connect working practices to project outcomes;
- trace key decisions across the full development lifecycle;
- evaluate strengths and weaknesses in their own process;
- audit final evidence without manufacturing missing evidence;
- prepare for finalisation and submission.

## Success criteria

### Developing
I can identify examples of decisions and working practices from my project.

### Secure
I can explain how my decisions, problem solving and organisation influenced the final website.

### Extending
I can synthesise evidence across design, development and review to make balanced judgements about my individual responsibility, creativity and self-management.

## Core teacher model

Use a generic events website or another non-assessed scenario.

### Weak claim

> I showed good self-management because I finished the website.

### Stronger evidence chain

> The developer identified that the application form was blocking completion of the main user journey, prioritised that defect ahead of cosmetic changes, recorded the fault, isolated the validation problem, implemented a fix and repeated the original tests. Lower-priority styling changes were deferred. The evidence therefore shows prioritisation and controlled problem solving rather than simply last-minute completion.

Ask learners to identify:

- action;
- evidence;
- decision;
- consequence;
- professional-practice quality demonstrated.

---

## Creativity discussion

Challenge the misconception that creativity means unusual colours or animation.

Purposeful creativity may include:

- developing an alternative layout to solve a usability problem;
- adapting a learned technique to a new requirement;
- designing original client-side behaviour;
- finding a simpler solution to a technical constraint;
- combining techniques appropriately;
- refining a design in response to evidence while retaining the core purpose.

The important questions are:

> What problem existed? What alternatives were possible? What did you decide? Why was your response appropriate?

---

## Individual responsibility discussion

Evidence might include:

- independently identifying a requirement gap;
- making and recording a design decision;
- debugging rather than relying on a supplied solution;
- deciding whether to accept, adapt or reject feedback;
- identifying a failed test honestly;
- taking corrective action and retesting;
- maintaining organised versions/evidence;
- recognising an unresolved limitation.

Avoid equating responsibility merely with working alone.

---

## Self-management discussion

Consider:

- planning milestones;
- prioritising important requirements;
- maintaining a usable file/folder structure;
- naming/versioning work sensibly;
- keeping backups/version history;
- testing throughout rather than only at the end;
- collecting evidence as work progresses;
- responding to setbacks;
- managing scope;
- completing essential functionality before optional enhancements.

---

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- use three evidence columns: **what I did / evidence / effect**;
- let learners identify artefacts before writing reflection;
- provide examples from the Hot Wheels development process without mapping them to Hot Beans answers;
- use oral conferencing to help learners retrieve genuine development events;
- separate design, development and review into three manageable sections.

### Stretch

Ask learners to:

- identify where their initial planning was ineffective and explain how they recovered;
- evaluate the quality, not just presence, of feedback;
- compare an independent decision with an alternative they rejected;
- recognise where evidence is weak or ambiguous;
- explain how a working practice affected the final product positively or negatively;
- distinguish creativity from unnecessary complexity.

---

## English and employability

Learners should use precise reflective language:

- evidence demonstrates;
- I prioritised;
- I rejected/adapted feedback because;
- this decision resulted in;
- a limitation of my approach was;
- next time I would;
- on balance.

Employability links include ownership, communication, debugging, prioritisation, documentation, quality assurance and professional reflection.

---

## Assessment-safe support

Use questions such as:

- Where in your project can you show that happened?
- What decision did **you** make?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- What happened because of your decision?
- What evidence exists from the time, rather than only your memory now?
- What would you manage differently next time?

Do not tell a learner that a particular action “proves BC.D3”. Assessment must consider the complete evidence.

## Expected learner outputs

By the end of the session learners should have:

- a professional-practice evidence map;
- at least one complete decision journey;
- a self-management review;
- a final evidence audit;
- a prioritised list of genuine remaining actions;
- reflective notes ready to integrate into final submission evidence.
