# Week 16 – Distinction Evaluation & Professional Practice

**Unit:** Pearson BTEC Level 3 Unit 6 – Website Development  
**Learning Aims:** B and C  
**Focus:** Distinction-level synthesis, individual responsibility, creativity, self-management and final evidence quality

## Week purpose

Week 16 brings together the complete design-development-review journey. Learners should no longer treat design, implementation, testing, optimisation and evaluation as separate tasks. Instead, they examine how their own decisions and working practices affected the quality of the final outcome.

The central synthesis is:

> **requirement → decision → action → evidence → consequence → reflection → judgement**

The repository's assessment-boundary guidance identifies **BC.D3** as concerning individual responsibility, creativity and effective self-management across design, development and review. This week therefore focuses on making authentic evidence of those qualities visible without manufacturing a retrospective story.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session learners should be able to:

1. explain what individual responsibility looks like in a website-development project;
2. distinguish meaningful creativity from decorative novelty;
3. identify evidence of independent problem solving and decision making;
4. evaluate the effectiveness of planning, organisation and time management;
5. evaluate how review, testing and feedback influenced decisions;
6. trace an important project decision from requirement to final outcome;
7. identify evidence gaps without inventing evidence;
8. make balanced judgements about their own professional practice;
9. identify realistic improvements to their development process;
10. prepare a coherent final evidence set for submission.

## Professional-practice themes

- ownership of decisions;
- independent problem solving;
- creativity linked to purpose;
- responding to feedback critically;
- organisation and file management;
- versioning and change control;
- testing discipline;
- evidence capture;
- prioritisation;
- time management;
- persistence and debugging;
- recognising limitations;
- professional reflection.

## Learning sequence

**retrieve → evidence audit → trace decisions → evaluate practice → identify gaps → refine → final readiness**

## Assessment connection

Week 16 explicitly strengthens the combined B/C distinction evidence, particularly the criterion concerned with individual responsibility, creativity and effective self-management across design, development and review. It also strengthens the quality of the final evaluation by connecting process decisions to outcomes.

Git history, screenshots and logs can support the story of development, but no single artefact automatically proves achievement. Evidence should be considered as a coherent whole.

## Assessment boundary

Do not provide learners with statements claiming that they demonstrated creativity, responsibility or self-management. Teacher support should help them locate and explain their own authentic evidence. The Hot Wheels exemplar may model processes, but the assessed evidence must come from the learner's own Hot Beans project.
