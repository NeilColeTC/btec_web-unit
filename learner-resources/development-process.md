# Development Process

The Hot Wheels exemplar is designed to make a development journey visible.

## 1. Investigate

Look at existing websites and identify how design and technical choices affect users. Consider purpose, audience, navigation, accessibility, content, media, performance and responsiveness.

## 2. Experiment

Use small examples to understand HTML, CSS and JavaScript techniques. At this point the aim is to learn what a technique does.

## 3. Study the exemplar progression

### Stage 1 – HTML Structure
Focus on content, semantics, navigation, images, links and forms.

### Stage 2 – CSS & Responsive Design
Compare the same pages after presentation and responsive layout have been introduced.

### Stage 3 – JavaScript & Refinement
Identify where client-side scripting adds purposeful behaviour and which programming constructs are being used.

## 4. Critique

Do not assume every exemplar choice is suitable for every website. Ask what you would retain, reject or replace for a different client, audience and purpose.

## 5. Start the assessed project independently

Return to the client brief rather than copying the exemplar. Identify requirements and produce your own design response, including alternatives and technical planning.

## 6. Select techniques

Choose HTML, CSS, assets and client-side scripting because they address your requirements. The exemplar is a source of learned techniques, not a page template.

## 7. Develop and record

Build the website progressively. Keep meaningful evidence of decisions, problems, versions and changes.

## 8. Test

Test functionality, compatibility and usability using appropriate conditions, data and users. Record expected and actual outcomes.

## 9. Optimise

Use test results and feedback to identify genuine improvements. Keep before/after evidence and retest changed features.

## 10. Evaluate

Judge the final design and optimised website against the original client requirements. Consider strengths, weaknesses, alternatives, evidence from development, and the effect of your own decisions and working practices.
