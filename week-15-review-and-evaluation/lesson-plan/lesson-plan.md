# Week 15 Lesson Plan – Review & Evaluation

**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Learning Aim:** C  
**Prior learning:** Requirements analysis, design review, implementation, formal testing, optimisation and retesting.

## Learning objectives

Learners will:

- distinguish evaluation from description;
- organise evidence around client requirements and user needs;
- make supported judgements about strengths and limitations;
- compare chosen approaches with alternatives;
- evaluate the effect of testing and optimisation;
- reflect on their own development process.

## Success criteria

### Developing
I can identify strengths and weaknesses and link them to requirements.

### Secure
I can use evidence from testing and feedback to justify judgements about fitness for purpose.

### Extending
I can evaluate trade-offs, alternatives and development decisions, then reach balanced conclusions supported by evidence.

## Core modelling sequence

Use a non-assessed example such as a generic college events website.

### Description

> The site has a responsive navigation menu.

### Explanation

> The responsive navigation menu makes links easier to access on smaller screens.

### Analysis

> The menu reduces the space occupied by navigation on small screens, allowing event content to remain prominent while retaining access to all sections.

### Evaluation

> The responsive menu was effective because mobile testing showed all sections remained reachable while the navigation occupied less screen space. This better supports users browsing quickly on phones. A persistent bottom navigation could have reduced interaction further, but it would have taken more permanent screen space and suited only a small number of destinations. On balance, the collapsible menu was the more appropriate choice for this content structure.

Ask learners what changed at each level.

---

## Evaluation questions to repeat

- What requirement are you judging?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- How did the decision affect the intended user?
- What limitation remains?
- What alternative could realistically have been used?
- What trade-off would that alternative introduce?
- What is your final judgement?

---

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- provide sentence frames built around evidence and consequence;
- allow bullet-point evidence mapping before extended writing;
- colour-code requirement/evidence/judgement in sample paragraphs;
- provide an evaluation vocabulary bank;
- use one requirement at a time rather than asking learners to evaluate the whole website at once.

### Stretch

Ask learners to:

- weigh conflicting evidence;
- discuss trade-offs rather than absolute success/failure;
- compare implementation decisions with credible alternatives;
- evaluate whether optimisation solved the original problem completely or partially;
- identify limitations in their own testing evidence;
- discuss how working practices influenced the outcome.

---

## English and maths

### English

Focus on evaluative connectives:

- however;
- although;
- therefore;
- consequently;
- whereas;
- on balance;
- this was effective because;
- a limitation is;
- an alternative would have been.

### Maths/data literacy

Where available, learners should interpret measurable evidence such as:

- viewport widths;
- file sizes;
- validation thresholds;
- performance measurements;
- number/proportion of tests passed before and after refinement.

Avoid inventing quantitative claims where no measurement exists.

---

## Employability and transferable skills

- evidence-based decision making;
- critical self-review;
- communicating technical decisions to non-specialists;
- acknowledging limitations;
- proposing realistic future development;
- professional reflection.

---

## Assessment-safe teacher support

Appropriate prompts:

- Which piece of your evidence best supports that claim?
- What did the user experience before and after the change?
- Is that a description or a judgement?
- What alternative did you reject, and why?
- What limitation remains?

Avoid supplying evaluative conclusions about the learner's own website.

## Expected learner evidence by end of session

Learners should have:

- a completed evidence map;
- several upgraded evaluative paragraphs;
- a structured final evaluation plan;
- a drafted section supported by evidence;
- peer feedback identifying unsupported claims or missing judgements.
