# Week 14 – Optimisation & Refinement

**Unit:** Pearson BTEC Level 3 Unit 6 – Website Development  
**Learning Aim:** C – Develop a website to meet client requirements  
**Focus:** Evidence-led optimisation, justified refinement and retesting

## Week purpose

Week 14 turns formal testing evidence into purposeful improvement. Learners use genuine defects, usability weaknesses, accessibility concerns, performance findings and user feedback to identify changes that make their own Hot Beans website more effective.

The key workflow is:

> **evidence → issue → priority → alternatives → justified change → retest → before/after comparison → judgement**

Optimisation is treated as an evidence-led process rather than a late-stage attempt to make the website look more polished.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session learners should be able to:

1. distinguish defect fixing from optimisation/refinement;
2. identify improvement opportunities from testing and feedback;
3. prioritise changes according to impact, urgency and effort;
4. consider alternative responses before selecting a solution;
5. justify refinements against user/client requirements;
6. preserve before-and-after evidence;
7. retest changed features using comparable conditions;
8. identify possible regression effects after a change;
9. judge whether an optimisation was actually successful;
10. explain how testing evidence influenced development decisions.

## Areas that may be optimised

Depending on the learner's own evidence, appropriate areas can include:

- navigation and information architecture;
- responsive layout;
- typography and spacing;
- accessibility and keyboard operation;
- form feedback and validation behaviour;
- JavaScript interactions;
- image dimensions/compression;
- page weight and load behaviour;
- content clarity;
- consistency;
- compatibility;
- user journeys;
- error prevention and recovery.

## Learning sequence

**retrieve → diagnose → prioritise → compare alternatives → refine → retest → compare → judge**

## Assessment connection

This week strengthens the evidence learners will need when explaining how their website was improved through testing and review. Strong evidence should make visible:

- the original weakness;
- the evidence that identified it;
- alternatives considered;
- the reason for the chosen response;
- the change made;
- the retest result;
- any side effects/regression checks;
- the resulting benefit to users/client requirements.

## Assessment boundary

The Hot Wheels exemplar may be used to model optimisation methodology. Learners should not reproduce its mobile-navigation issue simply so they have something to improve. Their evidence must arise from genuine testing, feedback and development of their own Hot Beans website.
