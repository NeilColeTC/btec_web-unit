## Slide 7 – Why alternatives matter
Professional design rarely begins and ends with one idea.

## Slide 8 – A real alternative changes something meaningful
Examples:
- navigation model
- information hierarchy
- page structure
- interaction pattern
- content emphasis
- responsive strategy

## Slide 9 – Cosmetic variation
Changing only colour or font while leaving structure unchanged is usually not a strong alternative.

## Slide 10 – Compare alternatives against requirements
Ask:
- which requirement does this address?
- who benefits?
- what is the trade-off?
- is it feasible?
- which option better supports purpose?

## Slide 11 – Alternative-design task
Develop one meaningful alternative to an existing design.

## Slide 12 – Responsive design
Responsive design adapts presentation and interaction to available space and device context.

## Slide 13 – Do not just shrink
A mobile design may need:
- stacked content
- simplified navigation
- larger touch targets
- prioritised content
- reduced visual clutter

## Slide 14 – Compatibility questions
- desktop?
- tablet?
- mobile?
- orientation?
- browser?
- input method?

## Slide 15 – Responsive annotation

## Slide 16 – SEO planning
Plan before development:
- page titles
- useful headings
- descriptive content
- keywords used naturally
- metadata
- meaningful link text
- image alt text

## Slide 17 – SEO is not keyword stuffing
Visibility depends on useful, structured, accessible content.

## Slide 18 – Asset planning

## Slide 20 – Original vs ready-made assets
Both may be appropriate, but provenance must be recorded.

## Slide 21 – Why plan JavaScript before coding?
Planning helps separate intended behaviour from syntax.

## Slide 22 – Pseudocode
Pseudocode describes logic in structured plain language.

## Slide 23 – Example neutral pseudocode
INPUT email
IF email is empty
DISPLAY error
ELSE
DISPLAY confirmation
END IF

## Slide 24 – Flowcharts
Flowcharts represent the logic visually.

## Slide 25 – Core symbols
- terminator: start/end
- process
- decision
- input/output
- flow line

## Slide 26 – Decision points
A decision should normally branch according to a condition.

## Slide 27 – Convert logic
Turn your pseudocode into a flowchart.

## Slide 28 – Copyright
Copyright protects original creative work.

## Slide 29 – Credit is not automatically permission
Attribution does not remove the need to consider copyright/licensing.

## Slide 30 – Data protection
If a website collects personal data, design decisions should consider what is collected and why.

## Slide 31 – Application forms
Ask:
- do we need this field?
- what will happen to the data?
- is the purpose clear?

## Slide 32 – Technical constraints

- browser/device support
- time
- asset size
- accessibility
- performance

## Slide 33 – Design traceability
Requirement → design choice → technical plan → evidence

## Slide 34 – From B.P2 toward B.M2
