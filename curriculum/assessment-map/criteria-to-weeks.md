## How to use this map

The table should not be read as a set of fixed submission dates. Instead it supports three planning questions:

1. **Has the underlying knowledge/process been taught?**
2. **Has the learner had a realistic opportunity to generate authentic evidence?**
3. **Is there a later consolidation point before submission?**

## Key curriculum checkpoints

### End of Week 4

Learning Aim A teaching is complete. Learners should understand the difference between comparison, analysis and evaluation and have had structured opportunities to review evidence against A.P1/A.M1/A.D1.

### End of Week 8

The principal Learning Aim B design cycle is complete. Learners should have requirements, design documentation, alternatives, review evidence, refinements and developing justification.

### End of Week 12

Learners should have a coherent implementation and should be able to trace major client requirements into working features. Major implementation gaps should be visible before formal testing begins.

### End of Week 14

Formal testing and evidence-led optimisation should provide a clear record of problems, changes and retests.

### End of Week 16

Learners should have organised the evidence needed to support final evaluation and professional-practice judgements. Genuine evidence gaps should be identified rather than concealed.

### Week 17

The emphasis is verification and submission readiness. It is not a replacement teaching or evidence-manufacturing week.

## Intervention principle

When a learner is behind, diagnose the missing **stage of the process**, not only the missing criterion. For example:

- weak B.M2 often indicates that design decisions were not linked clearly enough to requirements or alternatives;
- weak C.M3 often indicates insufficient before/after and retest evidence;
- weak BC.D3 often indicates that project decisions and self-management were not recorded as the work progressed.

Intervention should help learners expose their own reasoning and authentic evidence rather than provide criterion-ready prose.
