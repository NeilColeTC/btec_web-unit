# Week 8 – Design Review and Justification

**W/B:** 02 November 2026  
**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Specification focus:** B1 – Website design and review  
**Assessment preparation:** B.P3, B.M2

## Curriculum intent

Week 8 moves learners from producing design documentation to reviewing and refining it professionally. The emphasis is not simply on collecting comments, but on using structured feedback to make justified design decisions.

Learners should understand the progression:

**design → review → feedback → decision → refinement → justification**

The session prepares learners to review their own website designs with others, record useful feedback, identify appropriate improvements and justify why final design decisions meet user needs and the intended purpose.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

1. Explain the purpose of a structured design review.
2. Present website design ideas clearly to reviewers.
3. Collect specific, useful feedback from at least two reviewers.
4. Distinguish between useful feedback, preference and unsupported opinion.
5. Record accepted and rejected feedback with reasons.
6. Refine design documentation in response to appropriate feedback.
7. Explain how refinements improve fitness for purpose and user experience.
8. Begin producing the justification required for B.M2.

## Core evidence

Learners should leave the session with evidence including:

- review records from at least two people
- feedback linked to specific design elements
- decisions showing whether feedback was accepted, adapted or rejected
- revised design evidence
- before/after comparison evidence
- written justification linking decisions to user needs and purpose

## Formative assessment

Use:

- retrieval questioning
- review-readiness checklist
- observation of review conversations
- quality of reviewer comments
- feedback decision table
- before/after evidence
- justification writing
- exit ticket

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- reviewer question prompts
- feedback sentence stems
- accept/adapt/reject decision grid
- worked neutral example
- justification frame: **decision → reason → user need → purpose**
- checklist of evidence expected from the review process

### Stretch

Ask learners to:

- identify conflicting reviewer opinions
- prioritise feedback by impact rather than popularity
- explain why some feedback should be rejected
- distinguish aesthetic preference from usability evidence
- justify compromises between competing user or client needs

## Assessment boundary

Tutors should model the review process using a neutral scenario rather than supplying completed Hot Beans Web review evidence. Learners must conduct and document their own review, decisions and refinements.
