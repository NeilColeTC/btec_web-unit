# Week 8 Lesson Plan – Design Review and Justification

**W/B:** 02 November 2026  
**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Specification focus:** B1  
**Assessment preparation:** B.P3, B.M2

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Explain why website designs should be reviewed before implementation.
2. Present their design evidence in a form another person can understand.
3. Gather purposeful feedback from two reviewers.
4. Judge whether feedback should be accepted, adapted or rejected.
5. Refine designs and preserve evidence of the change.
6. Justify design decisions in relation to user needs and fitness for purpose.

## Key terminology

review, feedback, refinement, iteration, stakeholder, reviewer, usability, fitness for purpose, user need, requirement, justification, evidence, traceability, priority, constraint, compromise, alternative, annotation.

## Teacher modelling

Use a neutral example and demonstrate this sequence:

1. Show a clear design decision.
2. Ask the reviewer a focused question.
3. Record the reviewer's actual feedback.
4. Decide whether to accept, adapt or reject it.
5. Explain the decision using user need, purpose, requirement or constraint.
6. Show the revised design if a change is made.
7. Preserve before/after evidence.

A strong justification is not “I changed it because my reviewer said so.” It explains why the change is appropriate.

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- give learners a fixed review question bank
- allow paired oral rehearsal before presentation
- provide feedback stems such as “This may cause a problem because…”
- provide a three-column accept/adapt/reject decision grid
- provide a justification structure: **I decided to… because… This supports the user by… and helps meet the requirement to…**

### Stretch

- resolve conflicting feedback from two reviewers
- prioritise changes according to impact and feasibility
- justify rejecting popular but inappropriate suggestions
- identify design trade-offs introduced by a refinement
- distinguish between a requirement-driven change and a preference-driven change

## English and mathematics

**English:** professional discussion, concise feedback, comparative language, justification, evidence-based reasoning.

**Mathematics:** proportion and responsive dimensions may feature when explaining layout changes, spacing or breakpoint decisions.

## EDI / British Values / employability

Require respectful critique and reasoned disagreement. Discuss inclusive design and the importance of reviewers representing different user perspectives. Link design review to workplace practices such as client meetings, peer review, agile iteration and design sign-off.

## Misconceptions to anticipate

- all reviewer feedback must be implemented
- more feedback automatically means better design
- personal preference is the same as evidence
- B.P3 is satisfied simply by saying “my friend liked it”
- refinement means redesigning everything
- B.M2 means describing what was changed rather than justifying why
- a rejected suggestion does not need explanation

## Assessment boundary

Learners must generate their own review evidence. Tutor support should focus on review technique, questioning, evidence structure and reasoning, not on writing learner-specific conclusions.

## After the lesson

Check that each learner has two identifiable review records, a decision log, visible refinement evidence and at least one developed justification. Any missing evidence should be addressed before practical implementation begins.
