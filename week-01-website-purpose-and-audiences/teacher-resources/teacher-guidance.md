# Week 1 Teacher Guidance – Website Purpose and Audiences

## Curriculum position

This session introduces Unit 6 and begins Learning Aim A, specifically A1. It should establish the analytical habits learners will need for A.P1, A.M1 and A.D1 without prematurely teaching all of the design principles covered in Week 2.

The course calendar identifies BBC and Amazon as the Assignment 1 comparators. Use them for learner investigation, but use a different website for detailed teacher modelling so learners still have to construct their own assessed reasoning.

## Core teaching message

Website effectiveness is contextual. A feature is not automatically good because it is modern, attractive or familiar. Learners should repeatedly connect design evidence to:

**purpose + audience + requirements**.

Use the framework:

**Website → Purpose → Audience → Requirements → Features → Suitability**

## Suggested questioning

- What is this website actually trying to help the user do?
- What does the organisation want to achieve?
- Who is most likely to use it?
- Does it have more than one audience?
- What would matter most to that audience?
- Which feature provides evidence for your claim?
- Why is that feature appropriate here?
- Would the same design choice work equally well for another website?
- What is similar between BBC and Amazon?
- What is different, and can the difference be explained by purpose or audience?

## Baseline assessment guidance

Do not pre-teach before the baseline. Use it to identify:

- prior HTML/CSS/JavaScript experience
- ability to distinguish description from analysis
- understanding of audience and purpose
- confidence with comparative writing
- learners who may need vocabulary scaffolding
- learners who need technical stretch later in the practical phase

Do not attach grades to the baseline.

## Retrieval quiz answers

1. A: Understand the principles of website development; B: Design a website to meet client requirements; C: Develop a website to meet client requirements.
2. A website primarily focused on providing/publishing content or information, often with interactive/Web 2.0 features.
3. A website primarily supporting the promotion, sale or delivery of products and/or services.
4. The intended group or groups of users for whom the website is designed.
5. Accept suitable examples such as inform, communicate, entertain, promote, sell, provide services or enable interaction.
6. Something an intended user needs or expects from the website.
7. Something the commissioning organisation/client needs the website to achieve or provide.
8. Accept justified examples such as clear navigation, accessibility, fast loading, mobile compatibility or accurate information.
9. Analysis requires explaining the consequence or suitability of the feature in relation to audience, purpose or requirements.
10. Website → **Purpose → Audience** → Requirements → **Features** → Suitability.

Challenge responses should explain that suitability can only be judged against the needs of the intended audience and the purpose of the site.

## Common misconceptions

### “The target audience is everyone”

Ask learners to identify who is most likely to use a particular service, what they are trying to achieve and whether all users have equal importance to the organisation.

### “The client is the user”

Use an organisation commissioning a site for customers. The organisation's goals and the end user's needs overlap but are not identical.

### “Amazon is just a shopping website”

Encourage identification of secondary purposes and services while keeping the primary commercial context clear.

### “BBC is just a news website”

Encourage learners to recognise the breadth of content/services and multiple audience groups.

### “It looks professional”

Ask: Which observable choices make you say that, and why do those choices help this particular audience?

## Formative evidence to retain

Teacher may retain or note:

- baseline outcomes
- recurring misconceptions
- quality of comparison language
- exit-ticket confidence
- learners requiring support/stretch

Learner activity sheets are developmental rather than summative evidence.

## Assessment integrity

Assignment 1 requires learners to compare website design principles and progress towards analysis/evaluation. Week 1 should establish vocabulary and reasoning, not generate a completed assignment response. Avoid giving learners polished BBC/Amazon paragraphs that could be submitted directly.

## Preparation for Week 2

Review exit tickets before the next session. Week 2 should begin with retrieval of purpose, audience and requirements before introducing the full set of design principles. This keeps design principles anchored to suitability rather than taught as an isolated checklist.
