## Slide 6 – Why do websites exist?

Websites are created to achieve purposes.

Possible purposes include:

- informing
- communicating
- entertaining
- promoting
- selling
- delivering a service
- supporting a community
- enabling interaction

**Question:** Can a website have more than one purpose?

---

## Slide 7 – Content-based websites

A content-based website primarily provides or publishes content for users.

Content might include:

- news
- articles
- video
- audio
- reference information
- user-generated material

Modern content sites may also use **Web 2.0** features that allow participation, sharing or interaction.

---

## Slide 8 – Product and service websites

A product/service-based website supports an organisation in promoting, selling or delivering products and/or services.

Examples of functionality may include:

- product listings
- search and filtering
- accounts
- bookings
- shopping baskets
- payment
- customer support

A site can combine content, products and services.

## Slide 10 – Target audience

A **target audience** is the intended group or groups of users for whom a product is designed.

Audience characteristics might include:

- age profile
- interests
- goals
- technical confidence
- buying behaviour
- information needs
- accessibility requirements
- context of use

Avoid simply writing **“everyone.”**

---

## Slide 11 – User requirements

A user requirement describes something an intended user needs or expects from the website.

Examples might include:

- clear navigation
- readable content
- relevant information
- useful search
- accessibility
- mobile compatibility
- quick feedback after an action

The correct requirements depend on the audience and purpose.

---

## Slide 12 – Client requirements

The **client** is the person or organisation commissioning or responsible for the website.

Client requirements might include:

- communicating the organisation's identity
- promoting products/services
- increasing enquiries or sales
- providing accurate information
- supporting business processes
- maintaining content efficiently

User and client requirements can overlap, but they are not identical.

---

## Slide 13 – Requirement challenge

- fast page loading

- easy-to-find information
- mobile navigation
- strong calls to action
- accessible content
- easy content updates

For each answer: **justify it.**

## Slide 14 – Our analysis framework

Use this repeatedly:

# Website → Purpose → Audience → Requirements → Features → Suitability

Do not stop at:

> “The website has a search bar.”

Ask:

> Why is search useful for this audience, on this website, for this purpose?

---

## Slide 15 – Teacher model

We will analyse a website together.

Questions:

1. What kind of website is it?
2. What is its purpose?
3. Who is its audience?
4. What do users need?
5. What does the organisation need?
6. Which features respond to those needs?
7. Why are they suitable?

---

## Slide 16 – From description to explanation

### Description

> The website has a search box.

### Better explanation

> The search facility allows users to locate specific content quickly rather than navigating through multiple categories, supporting users who arrive with a clear information need.

The second statement explains **why the feature matters**.

## Slide 20 – Comparative writing

Useful comparative language:

- both
- similarly
- in contrast
- whereas
- however
- unlike
- compared with

A strong comparison identifies a meaningful similarity/difference **and explains why it matters**.

---

## Slide 21 – Comparison structure

Try:

**claim → evidence from both websites → similarity/difference → reason → audience/purpose connection**

Avoid producing two separate descriptions with no direct comparison.
