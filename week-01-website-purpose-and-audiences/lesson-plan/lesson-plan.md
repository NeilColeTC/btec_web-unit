# Week 1 Lesson Plan – Unit Introduction, Website Purpose and Audiences

**W/B:** 7 September 2026  
**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Specification focus:** A1  
**Assessment preparation:** A.P1

## Learning outcomes

Learners will be able to:

1. Describe the structure and assessment journey of Unit 6.
2. Distinguish content-based from product/service-based websites.
3. Identify website purpose and intended audience.
4. Distinguish user requirements from client requirements.
5. Identify website features that support purpose and audience.
6. Make an initial comparison of BBC and Amazon.

## Key terminology

website, web page, content-based website, product-based website, service-based website, Web 2.0, purpose, target audience, user, client, requirement, user-friendly, consistency, navigation, customisation, flexibility, feature, suitability.

## Teacher modelling

Use a deliberately non-assessment-specific website. Think aloud using:

1. **Website:** What kind of website is this?
2. **Purpose:** What is it trying to enable or achieve?
3. **Audience:** Who is most likely to use it?
4. **Requirements:** What do those users need? What might the client/organisation need?
5. **Features:** Which visible or functional features respond to those needs?
6. **Suitability:** Why do those features make sense for this audience and purpose?

Move learners away from statements such as “the website looks good” or “it is easy to use” unless they can identify evidence and explain why it matters.

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- provide visual examples alongside terminology
- use paired oral rehearsal before written comparison
- provide sentence stems: “The main purpose is…”, “The audience needs…”, “This feature supports the audience because…”, “Both websites…, whereas…”
- provide a partially completed comparison table
- chunk the analysis framework and check understanding after each step

### Stretch

- identify secondary purposes and overlapping audiences
- distinguish organisational/client needs from user needs
- consider how requirements can conflict
- compare how BBC and Amazon solve similar usability problems differently
- require explicit cause/effect reasoning rather than descriptive feature lists

## English and mathematics

**English:** technical vocabulary, comparative connectives, concise explanation, audience awareness and evidence-based reasoning.

**Mathematics:** light links through interpreting proportions, screen dimensions and quantities of information. Formal performance/data considerations are developed later.

## EDI / British Values / employability

Discuss how websites serve users with differing needs and backgrounds. Establish respectful critique. Introduce inclusive thinking as part of professional web practice. Link analysis of client and user requirements to the role of a junior web developer communicating with stakeholders.

## Misconceptions to anticipate

- every website has only one purpose
- the client and user are always the same person
- target audience means “everyone”
- a product/service website only sells physical products
- content-based websites cannot contain commercial features
- attractive appearance automatically makes a website suitable
- identifying a feature is the same as explaining its effectiveness

## Assessment boundary

Activities prepare learners for A.P1. Do not provide completed comparative assignment paragraphs for BBC and Amazon. Model the analytical process on a different website and require learners to develop their own evidence and reasoning.

## After the lesson

Use baseline, quiz and exit-ticket evidence to identify learners needing vocabulary/concept support. Carry common misconceptions into Week 2 retrieval practice.
