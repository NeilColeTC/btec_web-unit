# Week 1 – Unit Introduction, Website Purpose and Audiences

**W/B:** 7 September 2026  
**Duration:** 3 hours  
**Specification focus:** A1 – Purpose and principles of website products  
**Assessment preparation:** A.P1

## Curriculum intent

Introduce learners to Unit 6 and establish the conceptual foundation for analysing websites. Learners should understand that websites are designed for identifiable purposes, audiences and requirements, and that design choices should be judged in relation to those factors.

Week 1 begins preparation for Assignment 1: **Website Evaluation**. BBC and Amazon are used as contrasting teaching comparators, as identified in the course calendar and scheme of work.

A useful analytical framework for the session is:

**Website → Purpose → Audience → Requirements → Features → Suitability**

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

1. Describe the scope and assessment structure of Unit 6.
2. Distinguish between content-based and product/service-based websites.
3. Identify the intended purpose of a website.
4. Identify and characterise a website's target audience.
5. Explain the difference between user and client requirements.
6. Identify features that support a website's purpose and audience.
7. Begin comparing BBC and Amazon using appropriate technical vocabulary.

## Specification coverage

### A1 Purpose and principles of website products

Learners are introduced to:

- content-based websites and Web 2.0 technologies
- product and/or service-based websites
- target audiences, including different user profiles
- requirements such as user-friendly, consistent, navigational, customisable and flexible design
- the idea that design principles must be considered in relation to intended audience and purpose

Detailed treatment of usability, layout, accessibility, navigation, typography, consistency, content and media follows in Week 2.

## Formative assessment

Use:

- baseline assessment
- targeted questioning
- website classification
- purpose/audience matching
- BBC/Amazon analysis table
- paired explanation
- retrieval quiz
- exit ticket

## Adaptive teaching

### Support

- visual examples of contrasting websites
- vocabulary bank
- Website → Purpose → Audience → Requirements → Features → Suitability framework
- sentence stems for explanation and comparison
- paired oral rehearsal before written responses
- partially completed analysis tables

### Stretch

- identify primary and secondary purposes
- distinguish client requirements from end-user requirements
- identify overlapping audience segments
- explain why the same feature may be suitable for one audience but less suitable for another
- move from feature identification to cause-and-effect reasoning

## Assessment boundary

Week 1 is formative preparation for A.P1. BBC and Amazon can be investigated as the course comparators, but teaching should develop a transferable method rather than provide completed assignment prose. Learners remain responsible for producing their own comparative evidence.
